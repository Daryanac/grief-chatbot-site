How research changed Lantern.

This report documents how three rounds of survey feedback, prototype testing, and critique changed the project’s tone, trust language, navigation, visual direction, and product framing.

01 — Overview

An iterative design process, not a one-pass aesthetic exercise.

The final Lantern website developed through multiple rounds of research, critique, and redesign. Survey 1 focused on audience perceptions of AI grief support and what users needed in order to trust such a platform. Survey 2 came after the preliminary website and helped identify weaknesses in that implementation. Survey 3 evaluated later website directions and directly informed the final prototype.

Across the stages, feedback consistently favored a site that felt warm, trustworthy, emotionally clear, and less corporate or artificial.

02 — Survey 1

Trust, safety, and tone became foundational requirements.

Early responses showed skepticism about an AI voice chatbot for grief. Participants associated the concept with discomfort, emotional distance, or a lack of authenticity, which meant the website needed to build trust immediately and explain itself carefully.

Warmer toneComforting language was preferred over a purely clinical voice.
Visible boundariesSafety disclaimers and consent/privacy information needed to appear early.
Clear positioningUsers wanted explicit language that Lantern is supportive technology, not therapy or a real person.

Major concerns included privacy and data misuse, emotional dependence, inaccurate or insensitive responses, and the lack of human empathy. These findings made trust and emotional safety central design priorities rather than secondary disclaimers.

03 — Early draft

The first implementation organized information but did not yet feel like a finished product.

The earliest website draft functioned more as a collection of survey findings and key information than as a polished experience. There was no formal wireframe or finalized design guide. The page included an early hero, safety disclaimers, product explanation, and interface ideas, but it primarily served to organize initial research.

Check-in feedback pushed the project toward greater focus and clarity. The product naming and hero messaging needed revision, and the website needed to communicate the core voice-chatbot concept more directly. That critique led to more direct homepage framing and a stronger emphasis on what the product actually does.

04 — Survey 2

The preliminary website needed to feel less artificial and more human-centered.

Participants responded positively to websites that felt simple, organized, welcoming, and less corporate. They also valued easy navigation, visible safety features, and clear layout structure.

Reduce “AI-looking” visualsArtificial-looking artwork and generic layouts weakened trust.
Add warmthThe early color scheme felt too white, cold, or sterile for grief support.
Improve clarityUsers wanted less text density, better spacing, mobile friendliness, and clearer product explanation.

The redesign responded with warmer amber and purple accents, more breathing room, clearer navigation, stronger emotional framing, and more visible safety transparency.

05 — Survey 3

The later prototype established the strongest visual and structural direction.

77.8% of Survey 3 respondents preferred Website #1, making it the clearest signal for the final prototype direction.

Respondents liked the top navigation, welcoming and supportive feeling, emotional clarity of the headline, and ease of scrolling. Participants also responded positively to the purple palette and lantern imagery, describing the visuals as calming, readable, and more aligned with grief and comfort.

Remaining suggestions included making the main image look less obviously AI-generated, adding warmth through amber and purple accents, simplifying information, and keeping the experience mobile-friendly. The final direction preserved the stronger emotional hook and one-page navigation while strengthening trust-building sections and warmer visual cues.

06 — Final impact

Research changed both the product framing and the presentation.

First, emotional trust and safety became non-negotiable. Instead of presenting Lantern only as an innovative AI concept, the site frames it as sensitive technology that requires boundaries, consent, and clear explanation. Second, the research shifted the visual direction away from a colder, more artificial appearance toward a softer and more human-centered interface. Third, users repeatedly asked to understand immediately what Lantern is, who it is for, and how it works, which led to clearer framing and navigation.

Survey 1 established emotional and ethical concerns, Survey 2 refined the preliminary implementation, and Survey 3 guided the final prototype selection and visual direction.

07 — Conclusion

The research made the project more appropriate for its emotional context.

User feedback repeatedly showed that an AI project involving grief must feel trustworthy, emotionally careful, and visually sincere. The resulting design emphasizes warmer tone, visible safety language, clearer product explanation, stronger navigation, and a more deliberate visual identity. The research did not simply improve how the website looks; it changed how the project communicates responsibility and user needs.
